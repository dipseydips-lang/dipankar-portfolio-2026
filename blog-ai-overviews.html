Beyond the Blue Links: How to Get Cited by Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity in 2026

by Dipankar Mukherji·GEO / AEO·~8 min read·target keyword: how to rank in AI Overviews

For twenty years, SEO had one scoreboard: your position among ten blue links. That scoreboard still exists, but a second one now sits above it, and for a growing share of queries it is the only one buyers ever see. Google AI Overviews answer the question before the links appear. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini answer it without a results page at all.

Here is what almost nobody selling "AI SEO" will tell you plainly: this is not a separate discipline requiring a new agency and a new retainer. It is the same craft with the difficulty turned up. This guide covers what actually earns citations in AI answers, what is recycled SEO advice wearing a new acronym, and how I approach generative engine optimization (GEO) in the content I write.

First, how AI answers actually choose their sources

Whether you call it GEO, AEO (answer engine optimization) or LLM SEO, the mechanics reduce to two questions:

1. Retrieval: can the system find and parse you? AI Overviews draw heavily from pages already ranking well for the query and its variations. ChatGPT and Perplexity, when they browse, run searches and read the top results. Which means the unfashionable truth is this: classic SEO is the entry ticket. A page that cannot crack the top results is largely invisible to the machines summarizing those results.

2. Selection: are you the easiest credible sentence to quote? Once candidate pages are retrieved, the systems favor passages that answer the question directly: self-contained, specific and attributable. This is where most otherwise-good content loses. It ranks, but it buries its answers under warm-up paragraphs, so the machine quotes a competitor who got to the point.

The seven habits of content that gets cited

1. Answer first, elaborate second

Every section should open with the answer in one or two clean sentences, then earn the elaboration. Journalists call it the inverted pyramid. LLMs call it dinner. If a heading asks a question, the very next sentence must answer it in a form that survives being lifted out of context.

2. Write quotable units, not walls

AI systems extract passages, not pages. Keep the extractable unit tight: a claim, its number, its qualifier, in two to four sentences. A drafting test I apply to client work: could this paragraph be read aloud as a complete answer on its own? If not, it will not be quoted as one.

3. Put proprietary numbers where machines can see them

Generic advice gets summarized without attribution because a thousand pages say it. Specific, original data gets cited because only you say it. "Businesses should audit their sites regularly" is invisible. "In audits of sites that had been doing SEO for over a year, I typically find 12 to 18 unresolved indexation issues" is a citation waiting to happen. Every piece I write for clients carries at least one claim that exists nowhere else on the internet. That is the whole moat.

4. Use structure the machines parse natively

Clear heading hierarchies phrased the way people ask questions. Tables for anything comparative, because tables are the most extraction-friendly format on the web. Definition-style sentences for key terms. FAQ sections with genuinely different questions, not five rewordings of the target keyword. And schema markup (FAQ, HowTo, Product, Organization) so the meaning is machine-readable, not just human-readable.

5. Be an entity, not just a website

LLMs reason about entities: brands, people, products, and the claims connected to them. Strengthen your entity footprint with consistent naming everywhere, an about page that states plainly who you are and what you are known for, author bylines with real credentials, and presence on the third-party pages AI systems trust when triangulating legitimacy: directories, review platforms and industry publications.

6. Get mentioned where the models look

Answer engines lean on community and editorial sources: Reddit threads, industry forums, comparison sites, news coverage. A genuine, useful presence in the places your buyers ask questions does double duty. Humans see it now, and models cite it later. This is PR and community work rebranded, and it is now measurable in AI answers.

7. Keep freshness signals honest

Answer engines strongly prefer current sources for anything date-sensitive: pricing, comparisons, "best of" queries. A visible updated date backed by real updates, refreshed numbers and current screenshots keeps you in the candidate pool. A fake updated date on stale content is the fastest way to be filtered out of it.

What changes in how you measure

Rankings still matter, but add three questions to monthly reporting:

  • Presence: for your twenty money queries, does an AI Overview appear, and are you cited in it? Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity your buyers' questions and record who gets named. Screenshot everything monthly. This is the new rank tracking, and for now it is delightfully manual.
  • Branded demand: AI answers often convert invisibly. Someone reads a recommendation naming you, then searches your brand directly. Watch branded search volume and direct traffic as downstream evidence.
  • Quality of clicks: AI Overviews absorb shallow informational clicks. The visits that survive tend to be deeper in intent. Judge pages on conversions and pipeline, not raw sessions, or you will misread the shift as decline.

What not to fall for

The GEO gold rush has produced some confident nonsense. Skip: hidden prompt injections in your pages (platforms filter for manipulation, and it torches trust if surfaced), mass-generating "AI-optimized" pages (thin content is thin content on every scoreboard), and any vendor promising guaranteed placement in AI Overviews (nobody can, exactly as nobody could ever guarantee rankings).

Frequently asked questions

Is SEO dead now that AI answers questions directly?

No, it has been promoted. The systems answering questions choose their sources mostly from content that already wins at classic SEO. What has died is lazy SEO: the recycled listicle that added nothing was already struggling, and AI summarization finished the job.

Should I block AI crawlers from my site?

For most businesses, visibility in AI answers is distribution, and blocking the crawlers is declining the distribution. Publishers with paywalled content face a real tradeoff. A services or product business almost never benefits from being invisible where buyers ask questions.

How is GEO different from traditional SEO in day-to-day work?

Roughly 70 percent identical: technical health, intent mapping, authority, good content. The 30 percent that is new: answer-first structure, quotable passage design, entity building, third-party presence and a new measurement routine. It is an evolution of the writing craft, which is why writers who understand machines are suddenly valuable.

How long does it take to show up in AI answers?

If you already rank top five for a query, structural improvements can surface you in related AI answers within weeks of recrawling. If you rank nowhere, GEO timelines are SEO timelines: months of building relevance and authority first. There is no shortcut around being genuinely credible.
