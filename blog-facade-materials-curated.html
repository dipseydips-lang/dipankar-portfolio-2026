Common Façade Materials: Where Glass, Aluminium, Stone and Composite Systems Fit

Client work | Façade & Fenestration · Client work

Façade material selection is rarely a matter of choosing the material that looks best in a sample. Weight, span, climate, thermal performance, maintenance, fabrication, installation and budget can all change which system makes sense for a particular building.

Glass: daylight with technical trade-offs

Glazing can create a strong connection between interior and exterior spaces and support daylight strategies. At the same time, solar gain, glare, thermal performance, safety and cleaning requirements have to be considered. The glass specification should therefore be tied to the building's orientation and performance targets rather than treated as an isolated aesthetic choice.

Aluminium: flexible and widely adaptable

Aluminium is commonly used in curtain walling, windows, doors, rainscreen systems and composite assemblies because it can be formed into relatively precise profiles. The important questions are not simply whether aluminium is suitable, but how the selected system manages thermal movement, drainage, fixings, finishes and interfaces with other materials.

Stone: appearance, mass and anchoring

Natural stone can give a building a distinctive, durable exterior, but its weight and fixing strategy introduce engineering considerations. Panel dimensions, support conditions, joints, moisture and access for replacement need attention during design. The visual specification and structural approach should be developed together.

Composite panels and rainscreen systems

Lightweight cladding systems can support efficient installation and a wide range of finishes. Their performance depends heavily on the backing wall, cavity, fire strategy, joints, fixings and drainage path. Looking only at the face material can therefore give an incomplete picture of system performance.

How to compare materials

A useful comparison starts with the project's constraints. Consider climate, building height, fire requirements, structural movement, maintenance access, local fabrication capability and expected service life. The material is only one component of the decision. The complete façade build-up determines how the building envelope will behave in practice.

What this means for the reader

The useful answer depends on the situation, but the common thread is the same: decisions improve when the important constraints are understood before the final choice is made. Good industry content should make that decision easier, not simply add another page to the website.
