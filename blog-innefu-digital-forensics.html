Digital Forensics in Cyber Investigations: What Happens After an Incident?

Client work | Cybersecurity & Intelligence · Client work

When a security incident occurs, the immediate priority may be containment. The investigation that follows has a different purpose: establish what happened, preserve relevant evidence, understand the sequence of events and identify the systems or accounts involved.

Evidence has to be preserved correctly

Digital evidence can change through normal system activity. Investigators therefore need controlled acquisition and documentation processes that preserve the integrity of the material being examined. The exact approach depends on the device, operating system and investigation scope.

Build a timeline from multiple sources

A useful incident timeline may combine endpoint artefacts, authentication records, network logs, cloud activity and file metadata. No single source necessarily tells the complete story. Correlating events can reveal the sequence that connects an initial access event to later activity.

Deleted data is not automatically gone

Depending on the system and storage conditions, deleted files or traces of activity may remain recoverable. Recovery is not guaranteed, and the usefulness of recovered data depends on how it can be validated and connected to the investigation.

Attribution requires caution

Investigations can establish technical evidence about accounts, devices, infrastructure and activity. That evidence does not automatically prove who was physically responsible. Strong reporting distinguishes observed facts from conclusions that require additional evidence.

Reporting turns evidence into action

An investigation report should make the findings understandable to both technical and decision-making audiences. Clear timelines, evidence references, limitations and recommended actions help organizations move from incident response to remediation and future prevention.
