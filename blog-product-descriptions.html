Product Descriptions That Sell: A Copywriting System, Not a Vibe

by Dipankar Mukherji·ecommerce copywriting·~7 min read·target keyword: how to write product descriptions

Most product descriptions are written by the person with the least time, on the day the product ships, from the manufacturer's spec sheet. Then everyone wonders why the page converts at half the category average. Product copy is the highest-leverage writing in ecommerce: it is the last words a buyer reads before deciding, and there are hundreds of chances to get it right. Here is the repeatable system I use when rewriting product pages for stores, most recently across a catalog of roughly 25 SKUs where every page followed the same disciplined structure.

The structure: five blocks, always in this order

Block 1: The positioning sentence. One sentence that says what this is and who it is for. "A 400-GSM hooded bath towel sized for kids aged 2 to 8, woven for daily machine washing." A buyer who reads only this sentence should know if they are in the right place.

Block 2: The differentiators, maximum three. Not ten bullet points of everything true. The two or three things that make a buyer choose this over the tab they have open next door. If you cannot name the differentiators, that is a product problem wearing a copy costume.

Block 3: The objection paragraph. Every product has one silent question that kills the sale: will it fit, will it last, will it work with what I own, what if I hate it. Answer it directly, in the description, before the buyer has to hunt for a returns policy. Named objections convert; ignored ones bounce.

Block 4: The specifics, scannable. Dimensions, materials, compatibility, care, what is in the box. Table or tight bullets. This is where thoroughness lives so the prose above can stay human.

Block 5: The nudge. One line that resolves hesitation: stock reality, delivery speed, guarantee, or the use-case picture ("ships flat, assembles in ten minutes, moves house easily"). Not three exclamation marks. One honest reason to decide now.

Voice rules that separate copy from filler

  • Verbs over adjectives. "Premium quality" claims; "survives 300 wash cycles" demonstrates. Every adjective should be convertible into a fact, or cut.
  • Write to one buyer. A description aimed at everyone reads like a brochure. Pick the primary buyer from your reviews and analytics and write to that person's situation.
  • Steal the customer's words. Reviews and support tickets contain the exact phrases buyers use for benefits and worries. Copy that mirrors buyer language converts better and matches long-tail search naturally.
  • Read it aloud. If a sentence embarrasses you spoken across a shop counter, it does not belong on the page.

Where SEO fits without wrecking the copy

The target keyword belongs in the page title, the H1, the first block and the image alt text, naturally, once or twice. Long-tail variants live in the specifics block and FAQ, where they occur organically. What tanks product pages is not too few keywords. It is duplication: the same description across variants, or the manufacturer's text shared with forty competitors. Unique beats optimized, every time the two conflict.

A before and after, condensed

Before: "Our premium LED panel light offers superior illumination with high quality materials and elegant design, perfect for any modern space."

After: "A 40-watt LED panel that replaces a 400-watt conventional fixture, built for offices running lights ten hours a day. Flicker-free driver rated for 50,000 hours, so it outlasts roughly six years of workdays before you think about it again."

The first could describe ten thousand products. The second could only describe this one, which is precisely why it ranks, gets cited and sells.

Scaling it across a catalog

You do not hand-craft two thousand descriptions. You hand-craft the top revenue drivers, build a template per category from those, and use AI-assisted drafting under editorial review for the long tail, with the five-block structure enforced in the brief. The structure is the quality control. The human judgment decides what the differentiators and objections actually are, because no model knows your buyers better than your reviews do.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a product description be?

As long as the decision requires. A phone case needs 80 words. A standing desk needs 400 plus a comparison table. Considered purchases earn longer copy; commodity items punish it. Let the buyer's questions set the length, not a word-count rule.

Do product descriptions really affect SEO?

Directly, yes: unique descriptive copy is often the only indexable text differentiating your product page from every reseller of the same item. Indirectly, better copy improves engagement and conversion signals. It is one of the few projects that pays on both rankings and revenue simultaneously.

Can AI write my product descriptions?

AI can draft within a structure at scale, and should, for the long tail. It cannot know which objection actually kills your sales or which differentiator your buyers repeat in reviews. Feed it those human findings and edit its output, and you get scale without the sameness that makes AI catalogs invisible.
