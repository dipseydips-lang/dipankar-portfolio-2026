Keyword Research in 2026: Why Intent Beats Volume and How to Build a Map That Prints Briefs

by Dipankar Mukherji·SEO process·~7 min read·target keyword: keyword research process

Keyword research is where most content strategies quietly fail, months before anyone notices. The classic process, export a thousand keywords, sort by volume, chase the biggest, produces blogs that target queries the site cannot win, for searchers who will never buy. After years of doing this for agencies and clients, here is the process I actually use: smaller lists, intent first, business value scored, and every keyword attached to a page before anything is written.

The volume trap

Search volume is the most seductive and least useful number in SEO. Three reasons: high-volume terms are usually informational and dominated by entrenched authorities, so a newer site burns quarters chasing them; volume tools underreport long-tail and B2B queries dramatically, hiding the terms where deals actually live; and volume says nothing about value: "what is digital marketing" outweighs "digital marketing agency for manufacturers in Dallas" a thousandfold in volume and loses to it a thousandfold in revenue per visit. Volume is a tiebreaker, never the sort key.

Sort by intent, score by value

Every keyword answers to one question: what does the searcher intend to do next? Classify ruthlessly:

IntentQuery patternPage it deservesPriority
Transactionalbuy, price, quote, near me, for [industry]Service / product / location pageFirst
Commercial researchbest, vs, alternative, review, costComparison, buying guide, pricing pageSecond
Informationalhow, what, why, guideBlog, resourceThird, selectively
Navigationalbrand namesOnly yours matterHousekeeping

Then score each candidate on two axes: can we win it (look at who currently ranks; if it is all national giants and you are not one, be honest) and is it worth winning (does the searcher resemble a customer). The keywords scoring high on both are the plan. Everything else is a someday list.

Zero-volume keywords: the quiet goldmine

Some of the highest-converting pages I have written target queries the tools score at zero or near-zero: hyper-specific questions pulled from sales calls, support tickets and community threads. "Does [material] warp in coastal humidity" will never chart in a tool, and the eleven people a month who search it are all mid-purchase. In the AI answer era this matters double: assistants get asked exactly these long, specific questions, and they cite the pages that answer them completely. Build a standing pipeline from customer-facing teams to the keyword map, because your best keywords are spoken to your company before they are ever typed into Google.

SERP reading: the research the tools cannot do

Before committing to any keyword, search it and read the actual results, because the SERP is Google announcing what it believes the intent is. Check: what format wins (guides, product pages, videos, forums), whether an AI Overview appears and who it cites, what the top results miss (your angle lives in that gap), and whether the results are even about your meaning of the term. Ten minutes of SERP reading per priority keyword prevents the most expensive mistake in content: writing the right piece for the wrong intent.

The keyword map: where research becomes production

Research is finished only when it becomes a map: a sheet where every priority keyword has exactly one assigned URL (existing or planned), a search intent label, a format, and its cluster siblings. The map enforces the two rules that keep a site coherent: one page per intent (no two pages competing for the same query) and one intent per page (no page trying to serve three different searches and serving none). From a good map, briefs nearly write themselves, and the eternal "what should we publish next" meeting dies quietly. In my workflow, the research and mapping stage must complete before writing begins, because the most common bottleneck in content teams is not writing speed. It is writers waiting on research that should have existed first.

Frequently asked questions

Which keyword research tools do I need?

Any one mainstream tool for discovery and difficulty estimates, your own search console data for what you already almost rank for, and your customer-facing teams for the queries no tool sees. The stack matters far less than the sorting discipline applied to it.

How many keywords should a small business target?

Fewer than feels safe: a focused map of 30 to 80 priority terms across transactional and commercial-research intent, fully covered with excellent pages, will outperform a 2,000-row spreadsheet that never becomes anything. Depth of coverage beats breadth of ambition.

Has AI search changed keyword research?

It has shifted weight toward complete-question queries and toward being the citable source rather than merely the ranking one. The process above already points there: intent-first research and fully-answered specific questions are exactly what AI Overviews and assistants reward. The fundamentals moved up a level; they did not change.

How often should the keyword map be refreshed?

Quarterly for priorities and rankings, annually for a full rebuild. Markets shift language, competitors move, and search console accumulates evidence about what you nearly rank for, which is the cheapest opportunity list in all of SEO.
